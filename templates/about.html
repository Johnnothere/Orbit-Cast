About OrbitCast

A London event engine that reads your CV, scores every upcoming event against it, and shows you only the ones that clear the bar — including, often, none.

Why it says no

Most recommendation tools are built to always return something. Ask for five results and you get five, whether or not the fifth is worth your evening. That is a pleasant product and a useless one: once you have been to two events that were a stretch, you stop trusting the list.

OrbitCast scores fit from 0 to 100 and shows nothing below 65. It names what is thin in your profile as well as what is strong, caps the list rather than padding it, and when a quiet week produces no match it sends a short email saying exactly that. The scoring prompt, the threshold and the self-critique pass that catches drift back toward flattery are the product. Everything else is plumbing.

It started as a scraper and a spreadsheet. The hard part was never fetching pages — it was deciding what counts as a real event, and then being honest about fit.

Some of what that took, in no particular order: about half the catalogue showed “time TBC” against events whose own page stated a time, because listings carry a date and a link while the time lives one click deeper — so every event without one now has its page read once, deterministically, with no model involved. Several organisers sit behind Cloudflare and returned 403 to every scrape, repeatedly, so refused hosts are now paused and backed off rather than retried into the ground. And every database query was crossing a continent: the app runs in Europe and the database was in Tokyo, which is a quarter of a second per round trip against about ten milliseconds from London.

None of that is visible in the product. That is roughly the point.
